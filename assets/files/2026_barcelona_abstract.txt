RECENTERING DOMESTIC WORK : THE MEDIEVAL JURISTS - Despite a certain marginality in legal-historical scholarship, “domestic work” was anything but peripheral in late-medieval legal systems. The inherited Roman notion of work, “operae”, occurs chiefly in the Digest title De operis libertorum (D. 38.1), where it is treated as an asset deeply inherent to the Roman household. Drawing on a body of late-medieval scholastic writings known as the distinctiones operarum, this paper builds on this premise. It asks what “domestic work” meant for late-medieval jurists and, more broadly, what role it played in the economies of their times. The “house” did not simply denote a range of working activities. Rather, it functioned as a far broader institution for valuing and regulating work at large.
